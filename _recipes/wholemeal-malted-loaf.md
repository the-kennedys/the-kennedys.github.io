A hearty, nutty everyday loaf with a great crust and soft crumb that toasts brilliantly. Note that 6g is quite a low-salt recipe—feel free to push it up to 10g if you are normal.

On the kit front, I use a mahoosive mixing bowl, an oval Dutch oven from Nuovo, a dough scraper, and a lame (a razor blade on a handle). Two practical rules before you begin: keep a small bowl of water nearby to wet your hands so the dough doesn't glue itself to your fingers, and lay out three teaspoons on the counter—flip one upside down after each round of stretch-and-folds, because remembering which round you're on is otherwise impossible.

# Ingredients

- 250g strong wholemeal bread flour
- 250g malted bread flour (e.g. Granary or malted wheat flake)
- 330ml lukewarm water
- 1 tbsp olive oil 
- 1 tbsp clear vinegar
- 7g fast-action dried yeast (1 sachet)
- 6g salt

# Method

- Autolyse. Mix the flours with the water and vinegar into a rough dough in a BIG mixing bowl. I iniitially mix and bind with a knife for a few seconds, then work all the dry flour in using the dough scraper. Leave for 30 minutes to hydrate the bran.
- Add yeast, salt, and oil. Sprinkle the yeast and salt over the dough and drizzle with the olive oil. Wet your hands, press them into the dough, then fold in until incorporated. Leave for 30 minutes.
- First stretch and fold. Wet your hands. Stretch the dough up on the far side, fold over to the centre, turn the bowl 90 degrees, and repeat so you stretch on 4 "sides". Turn teaspoon #1 upside down. Leave for 30 minutes.
- Second stretch and fold. Same again: four corner stretches, turn teaspoon #2 upside down. Leave for 30 minutes.
- Third stretch and fold. Same again: four corner stretches, turn teaspoon #3 upside down. Leave for 30 minutes.
- Shape batard. On a plain floured work surface, stretch into a rectangle. Fold a third over to the middle from each side (like a letter). Roll down from the top and shape tightly into a batard, then drop into a rice-floured banneton, with the seam side up (best surface down). Set the oven to 220C and put the Dutch oven in. Wait 30 minutes.
- Flip the loaf onto a rice-floured sheet of parchment, and use a lame (or razor blade) to cut a 1 cm slice along the length. Use the parchment paper as a sling to get the dough into the Dutch oven.
- Bake with the lid on for 25 minutes to trap the steam.
- Remove the lid, reduce the temperature to 200C, and bake for another 15 minutes.
- Cool. Turn out immediately onto a wire cooling rack and leave to cool completely before slicing.
